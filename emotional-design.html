Research Area

Emotional Design

Contemporary trends in design show consumer inclination towards objects which motivate them, improve their quality of life and spark emotions. Consequently, along with the functionality requirements, a product needs to serve consumers' emotional needs, and for that, it must relate to them on an emotional level.

My current work investigates how products elicit specific emotional responses such as pleasure, surprise, interest, trust and how these responses can be anticipated and designed for rather than left to chance. The aim is to build frameworks that help designers move beyond usability and aesthetics alone, toward products that support meaningful emotional experiences across use, ownership, and disposal.

Focus Areas

Emotional response frameworks

Adapting and extending established models of product emotion to analyze the relationship between emotion, consumer behavior, and product design

Emotion-led design methods

Developing tools and workshop formats that help design students and practitioners translate emotional intent into concrete design decisions.

Evaluation of emotional experience

Using qualitative and mixed-method approaches to assess how users emotionally engage with product concepts and finished designs.
